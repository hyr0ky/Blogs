## Report

###  Summary  
While looking for pipeline vulnerabilities I accidentally discovered a way to make it impossible for targeted Slack users to use Chatops functionality, either Gitlab for Slack app or slash commands integration.

Not that critical, I think... but technically worth reporting.

###  Steps to reproduce 

1. Create a project on GItlab.com  
2. Go to settings and integrations, find Mattermost slash notification integration and click configure  
3. Activate it and set the token to "hackerone"  
4. Copy the Request URL  
5. Go to  a command line and do the following:  
```  
curl https://gitlab.com/api/v4/projects/YOUR_PROJECT_ID/integrations/mattermost_slash_commands/trigger -d "token=hackerone&user_id=TARGET_SLACK_ID&team_id=TARGET_SLACK_TEAM_ID&response_url=example.com&command=/gitlab&text=test&user_name=Gitlab.com&team_domain=Gitlab.com"  
```   
7. Open the URL in the response (example:   
```Hi there! Before I do anything for you, please \u003chttps://gitlab.com/-/profile/chat_names/new?token=B8mxwpUMxsEpnv5pq5J49Eye6prYiV9t-asdfadsf|connect your GitLab account```)  
8. Approve it  
9. Go to https://gitlab.com/-/profile/chat_names and observe you have the account linked  
10. Whenever the targeted slack user attempts to use any chatops functionality, they will not be able to log in nor remove your account from their slack account  
11. You can test this on your own slack account by simply copying your team id in the url of the workspace and copying your user id  
```  
Click on your profile picture.  
Click "View full profile"  
Click on "more" and click "Copy member ID"  
```

###  Impact  
Targeted DoS for chatops.

###  Components affected  
Gitlab and Slack users
